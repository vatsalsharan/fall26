What are things you found interesting about the papers for this class?

Lin et al. discuss the process of creating a differentially private corpus of synthetic data with privacy guarantees without actually training a model. What I find most interesting is the method behind how they make this happen. The foundation model never receives the private training examples directly, and is only used to generate synthetic "candidates". There is no fine tuning, and they essentially sacrifice all notion of computational efficiency to create a guided zero-shot synthetic data generator. It is a very creative idea that approaches privacy differently. The thing i find most interesting about the work of Carlini et al. is how strictly they consider the terms for "leaking" training data. If a picture of a famous person is reproduced, and their identity is very clearly differentiable, it may not be considered as a memorized sample unless it closely matches one single image from the training corpus. I think this means that the results may be understated by default, as combining multiple images of the same person to yield a recognizable result is not ideal, and yet is not counted as memorized.

What questions do you have about the papers for this class? What do you hope to learn from the discussion?

From Lin et al. I am more interested to know the degree of effect that hiding the private data from the foundation model actually has. A worthwhile experiment might be to see the quality of outputs between the foundation model seeing and not seeing examples from the private corpus to understand the difference in output quality and the degree to which privacy is guaranteed. In other words, I want to know how much of the synthetic data's usefulness actually comes from the original private data vs the knowledge of the foundation model by default. I am interested to understand how much of the models' learning capability is mistermed as memorization and vice versa. Is it necessary for part of the model to memorize certain features to become useful? If so, how much memorization is a necessity to yield useful results. Additionally, at what point does it become a security risk and requires containment? Is there a goldilocks zone within which diffusion models must operate to be real-world accessible without concern?

Junyang Cai

Oct 12, 2026

What are things you found interesting about the papers for this class?

I found it interesting that the two papers show almost opposite sides of generative models and privacy. The diffusion-model extraction paper demonstrates that diffusion models can memorize and reproduce individual training images, including photographs of people and copyrighted logos. The authors extracted more than a thousand training examples and found that diffusion models were less private than GANs under their evaluations. In contrast, the Private Evolution paper uses foundation-model APIs as a tool for privacy-preserving synthetic data generation. Instead of fine-tuning a model on private data, PE starts with generated samples and gradually moves the synthetic distribution toward the private distribution using a differentially private nearest-neighbor voting process. I found this contrast especially interesting: the same class of powerful generative models that creates privacy risks can also be used as part of a privacy-preserving pipeline.

What questions do you have about the papers for this class? What do you hope to learn from the discussion?

One question I have is how much the privacy guarantees of synthetic-data methods depend on the privacy of the underlying foundation model itself. I hope to learn more about how we should evaluate the privacy of synthetic data beyond simply having a formal DP guarantee.

What are things you found interesting about the papers for this class?

DIFFERENTIALLY PRIVATE SYNTHETIC DATA paper: It's an interesting paper that has use cases for the post-processing DP property we learned in the last class. It's very hard to adapt existing algorithms to support DP in their outputs, but by just using the API and making the outputs DP during inference, this has great applications for everyone consuming them downstream. Especially in cases like medical scans, where patient data is highly sensitive, we should still be able to generate data using a private set with DP guarantees, and everything downstream that uses this generated data has the same DP guarantees, which is very interesting. But they also present a key finding in their paper, i.e., even if the distributions of the generator model and the private dataset are far apart, the generated outputs still contain useful information, and classification models trained on them still produce non-trivial results. This suggests that steering the outputs through voting can still produce synthetic images that retain key features needed, even if the generator models aren’t explicitly trained on it. Extracting Training Data from Diffusion Models paper: It's not too surprising that they found diffusion models to memorize training data, as we have seen similar cases with LLMs and other models in class. But it's surprising that diffusion models are the least private among generative models they tested. In the process of generating an image by denoising, I initially assumed it would make them the most private, but the results suggest they leak twice as much as GANs do, and the paper also helped me understand the difference between randomness and privacy guarantees. I also like the way they find duplicate images in the Stable Diffusion dataset by first embedding all images with CLIP and then applying L2 to similar images found using the embeddings, rather than applying L2 to the entire 160 million-image dataset, to narrow down the search for memorized images. It's also surprising that applying existing methods like DP-SGD caused training to diverge in their experiments, even at high epsilon values of 50, suggesting that diffusion models might need a new approach to achieve DP guarantees at the very least in their case.

What questions do you have about the papers for this class? What do you hope to learn from the discussion?

DIFFERENTIALLY PRIVATE SYNTHETIC DATA paper: One main question I have for the paper is how one would ensure that synthetic images generated using the private dataset and voting-based steering are not overly generic. Since each image only gets one vote, if a rare class is underrepresented in the dataset, PE would never generate it. Another question I have is regarding when the distribution difference occurs between how the generative model is trained and the private dataset used. If they are too different, the paper reports lower results compared to SOTA, but they only tested it on the classification task. But other tasks, which are harder, like segmentation, etc., where they need nuanced features, might show a better understanding of this, as they require more details and might show the gap even better. Extracting Training Data from Diffusion Models paper: To what extent is the discrepancy between diffusion models and GANs due to the training objective rather than model capacity or training time? Would diffusion models still leak more in a comparison that more closely controls these factors? I hope to discuss and learn more about advisable methods, such as PE, for these foundational models, since including them directly during training requires much more effort than plug-and-play solutions.
