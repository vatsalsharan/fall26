Mohammadsaeed Haghi

Oct 9, 2026

What are things you found interesting about the papers for this class?

For the extraction paper, what I found most interesting is their explanation for why the divergence attack works at all. During pre-training, documents get packed together and separated by a special token, so the model learns that everything before it can be ignored. OpenAI blocks that token from being sent through the API. Their hypothesis is that repeating one word a few hundred times puts the model into a similar state without it. So the attack reconstructs a signal you are not allowed to send. For the RMIA paper, what I found most interesting is that their framework explains its own result. They rewrite the prior attacks as special cases of their test: LiRA turns out to be the average of their likelihood ratio over all population samples, and Attack-R drops the calibration against population data. LiRA needs many reference models because of that averaging. So their offline attack beats LiRA running online, as long as LiRA is not given hundreds of reference models.

What questions do you have about the papers for this class? What do you hope to learn from the discussion?

For the RMIA paper, Since the lack of reference models for deployed LLMs like GPT reduces RMIA to Attack-P, can substituting a cheap proxy for Pr(x) successfully recover RMIA's original performance?

Instead of only asking whether the target model fits x better than the reference models, it also checks whether that relative advantage exceeds that observed for random population points. In the CIFAR-10 comparison, offline RMIA even outperformed online LiRA in AUC until LiRA had hundreds of reference models.
