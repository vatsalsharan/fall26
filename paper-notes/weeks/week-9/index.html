What are things you found interesting about the papers for this class?

Scalable Extraction of Training Data from (Production) Language Models: 1. Study has large-scale implications which are alarming, but memorization can also be used for good! Although GPT claims they don't train their models on given derivations of GPT, we could use privacy audits to actually legally confirm this possibly! 2. Drawing a parallel to "Sleeper Agents" such vulnerabilities (whether backdoor or privacy in this case) are hard to confirm and may easily lie dormant in a model Low-Cost High-Power Membership Inference Attacks: 1. I found the paper’s framing of non-membership as many possible worlds particularly interesting. By comparing a candidate against both reference models and ordinary examples, RMIA makes privacy auditing practical with as few as one reference model.

What questions do you have about the papers for this class? What do you hope to learn from the discussion?

Scalable Extraction of Training Data from (Production) Language Models: 1. The intention behind "discoverable memorization" is to provide an upper bound on memorization, but given the discrepancies between models how useful would this experimentally found upper-bound apply to other models/datasets; are we assuming similar models trained on similar datasets will obey this upper-bound? a) Basically what is a principled approach to privacy auditing? 2. Deeper explanation on the Good-Turning method + its intuition would be helpful! 3. Optimal prompts from "Private Investigator: Extracting Personally Identifiable Information from Large Language Models Using Optimized Prompts" may derive a better upper-bound on memorization but is there a more principled approach to this? Low-Cost High-Power Membership Inference Attacks: 1. Although this method allows for a reduced budget, does it actually make privacy auditing more feasible?

Sheryl Mathew

Oct 2, 2026

What are things you found interesting about the papers for this class?

Scalable Extraction: The authors propose the definition of Discoverable memorization to complement Extractable memorization. They use open source models and efficiently query the models' training data to verify the success of various extraction attacks, which prior work was not able to do at scale. MIA: This paper presents a more realistic way to test for MIA.

What questions do you have about the papers for this class? What do you hope to learn from the discussion?

Scalable Extraction: It's not clear why larger and more capable models are more vulnerable to data extraction attacks. I thought it would be the other way around? MIA: I'm hoping to understand more about the intuition behind why the paper's statistical test is better than prior work based on the MIA game.
