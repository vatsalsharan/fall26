What are things you found interesting about the papers for this class?

I thought this paper's connections to economics and game theory were very interesting. For example, many points (especially the traffic example) reminded me of Nash's equilibrium and game theory as a whole. I also thought it was very interesting how models can actually reinforce biases and even create some because of how they validate patterns that can statistically justify unfairness. This is a particularly concerning thought, because if the future is shaped by algorithmic thinking, then anyone could steer what patterns the model observes, reinforces, and creates, and it would be very easy to convince the public in general that the model's behavior was fair and justified. Lastly, I thought it was really cool how they were able to take a complex issue and formalize it mathematically; this isn't something I had ever even really thought about before.

What questions do you have about the papers for this class? What do you hope to learn from the discussion?

Based on the point about manufacturing fairness gaps, I'm very interested to hear what people have to say about potential solutions for this. I am also curious to discuss how we as researchers can prevent this, since it seems very easy to diminish the perceived role of a model in contributing to unfair societal systems, since it could be argued that new data simply validate existing patterns of behavior. I would also like to hear whether there are interpretability/explainability solutions here since a very critical part of preventing an algorithmically driven unfair society is making sure people have access to transparent information about how models are trained and make decisions.
